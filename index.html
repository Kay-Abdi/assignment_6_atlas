Stoner

by John Williams

Stoner follows an ordinary man through an ordinary life. William Stoner grows up in the 20th century, becomes an English professor, loves, fails, teaches, and moves through decades with quiet persistence. By the end, you grieve for a person who never existed but felt completely alive as you read through their experience. That's the power of this book.

Why It Matters

Williams writes with rich prose that makes the mundane profound. He doesn't look away from difficulty. Stoner's relationship with literature becomes his relationship with meaning itself, and through that we see how reading and teaching can sustain a life. But the book also doesn't shy away from regret, from the choices we make and the paths we don't take, from loving the wrong person or at the wrong time.

It's a book about how to live with regret, how to find value in ordinary moments, and how literature can be the thing that keeps you human when everything else fails.

This is my favorite book of the year.
