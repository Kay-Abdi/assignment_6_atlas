Stoner

by John Williams

Stoner is a quiet masterpiece about a man's life unfolding across decades at a university. William Stoner is an English professor whose life seems unremarkable on the surface, but Williams traces the depth of his inner world with profound care.

Why This Book Matters

Published in 1965, Stoner was largely overlooked for decades before experiencing a resurgence in recent years. The novel captures something essential about what it means to be alive, to love imperfectly, to teach, to fail, and to find meaning in quiet moments.

What I Love About It

  • The writing is beautiful without being showy
  • Stoner's relationship with literature and teaching feels authentic
  • The complexity of his marriage is unflinching
  • It asks real questions about vocation and regret
  • The ending is devastating but peaceful

Who Should Read It

If you're someone who thinks about what matters and reads for language and feeling, then Stoner is for you. This is my favorite book of the year.
